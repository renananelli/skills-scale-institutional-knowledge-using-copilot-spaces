# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

This repository centralizes OctoAcme's project management practices and operational guidance for cross-functional work. It provides a shared source of truth for how the organization initiates work, plans delivery, tracks execution, manages risks, communicates status, and improves its processes over time. The goal is to give teams a consistent framework for turning customer and business needs into well-scoped, transparent, and measurable outcomes.

OctoAcme follows a lightweight, stage-based lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective. During initiation, teams validate the problem, confirm stakeholders, define success metrics, and produce a project one-pager or charter before moving into planning. Planning turns the approved initiative into a prioritized backlog, a clear Definition of Done, milestone-based timeline, and a visible list of risks and dependencies. This keeps teams aligned on scope, ownership, and expected value before work begins.

The organization defines clear roles and responsibilities to support accountability and collaboration. Product managers define outcomes and backlog priorities, project managers coordinate schedules, dependencies, and communication, developers build and test the solution, QA validates quality and acceptance criteria, and stakeholders provide input and approval at key checkpoints. The communication model is cadence-based: weekly PM and product syncs, twice-weekly standups for delivery teams, monthly stakeholder updates, and escalation paths when blockers or business-impacting issues arise. This structure creates visibility while preserving a simple decision path.

During execution, OctoAcme emphasizes iterative delivery, issue-based tracking, and quality by default. Work is organized in a project board with statuses such as Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, and Done, and pull requests are expected to include context, acceptance criteria, and automated validation before merge. Quality practices include unit and integration testing, smoke tests for critical user flows, security scanning, and manual validation when needed. Release work follows strict pre-deployment readiness checks, smoke tests, rollback planning, and post-deploy verification to reduce operational risk. After each sprint or milestone, teams conduct retrospectives and capture action items to improve the process continuously.
